Abstract {#abstract-1}
----------------------

**Background** Fibrostenosis is a common and disabling complication of Crohn's disease (CD) with no reliable early biomarkers, limited treatment options and high postoperative recurrence.  
**Objective** To investigate the contribution of glioma-associated oncogene homolog 1 (GLI1+) mesenchymal cells to fibrostenotic progression and their potential as a therapeutic target.  
**Design** Fibrotic and non-fibrotic tissue samples were collected from patients with CD undergoing surgery for fibrostenotic obstruction. Chronic colitis was induced in mice using dextran sodium sulfate (DSS) or 2,4,6-trinitrobenzene sulfonic acid (TNBS). Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on human and murine intestinal tissues. *Gli1-CreERT2; R26-tdTomato* and *Gli1-CreERT2; R26-iDTR* mice were used for lineage tracing and targeted cell ablation. GLI1+ mesenchymal cells were isolated to identify pathogenic determinants. A surgical CD fibrostenosis cohort was analysed.  
**Results** Single-cell transcriptomics and immunofluorescence revealed enrichment of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells at fibrotic sites. Lineage tracing showed that these cells expanded and adopted a myofibroblast phenotype in chronic colitis, whereas their ablation alleviated fibrosis. GLI1+ mesenchymal cells exhibited a distinct transcriptional expression profile with prominent expression of SPARC-related modular calcium-binding protein 2 (SMOC2). SMOC2 was upregulated in human and murine fibrosis, and its knockdown in vivo and in vitro attenuated fibrosis. Mechanistically, GLI1+ mesenchymal cells secreted SMOC2, promoting extracellular matrix deposition and fibroblast activation. In surgical CD cohorts, high SMOC2 expression in intestinal tissue correlated with postoperative recurrence, confirmed in the validation cohort.  
**Conclusion** GLI1+ mesenchymal cells drive intestinal fibrostenosis through SMOC2-mediated niche formation. SMOC2 may serve as a predictive biomarker for postoperative recurrence and a potential therapeutic target for CD.

Introduction {#sec-9}
---------------------

Crohn's disease (CD) is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the GI tract, with high prevalence in developed countries and increasing incidence in developing regions.[1 2](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-1){#xref-ref-1-1} Intestinal fibrosis, a major complication of CD, results from excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, leading to strictures in the intestine.[3](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-3){#xref-ref-3-1} No effective antifibrotic therapies are currently available, and most patients ultimately require surgery, with recurrence in up to half within 10 years.[4--7](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-4){#xref-ref-4-1} The molecular drivers of fibrostenotic progression remain poorly understood, and current treatments do not effectively target fibrogenesis.[8--10](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-8){#xref-ref-8-1}{#p-11}

Among the pathological features of CD progression, dysregulated activation of stromal cells and excessive deposition of pathological ECM drive fibrostenosis development.[11 12](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-11){#xref-ref-11-1} Recent single-cell studies have revealed heterogeneous stromal populations within strictures, including stromal subsets such as FAP+[13](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-13){#xref-ref-13-1} and CDH11+[14](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-14){#xref-ref-14-1} cells that contribute to fibrogenic ECM deposition.[15](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-15){#xref-ref-15-1} However, the functional hierarchy and regulatory crosstalk among these stromal populations remain largely undefined.{#p-12}

In this context, glioma-associated oncogene homolog 1 (GLI1+) mesenchymal cells have been proposed as key contributors to fibrogenesis.[16](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-16){#xref-ref-16-1} GLI1 marks perivascular, stem cell-like mesenchymal populations with trilineage differentiation potential across multiple organs.[16 17](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-16){#xref-ref-16-2} Emerging evidence indicates that GLI1+ mesenchymal cells play critical regulatory roles in fibrogenesis in the kidney,[18](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-18){#xref-ref-18-1} bone marrow,[19](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-19){#xref-ref-19-1} lung,[20](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-20){#xref-ref-20-1} liver[21](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-21){#xref-ref-21-1} and heart.[22](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-22){#xref-ref-22-1} Within the intestine, GLI1+ mesenchymal cells reside near crypt niches and modulate stem cell dynamics via WNT signalling crosstalk.[23 24](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-23){#xref-ref-23-1} Although their role in maintaining epithelial homeostasis has been established, the specific contribution of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells to intestinal fibrosis remains unclear.{#p-13}

Here, we demonstrate that GLI1+ mesenchymal cells undergo pronounced expansion and drive excessive ECM deposition in CD-associated fibrostenosis, as revealed by murine lineage tracing. We further identified SPARC-related modular calcium-binding protein 2 (SMOC2), a matricellular protein selectively enriched in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells, as a pivotal mediator of intestinal fibrosis. Importantly, increased SMOC2 expression in CD fibrostenosis correlated with an elevated risk of postoperative recurrence, underscoring its potential as a prognostic biomarker. Collectively, these findings reveal a previously unrecognised profibrotic role for GLI1+ mesenchymal cells and establish SMOC2 as both a candidate therapeutic target and a clinical predictor of CD-associated fibrostenosis.  

Methods {#sec-10}
-----------------

### Human intestinal specimens {#sec-11}

Paired surgical resection specimens from the strictured segment and adjacent non-fibrotic terminal ileum of 134 patients with fibrostenotic CD were included. Fibrosis was confirmed preoperatively by radiographic imaging and/or ileocolonoscopy and postresection by an experienced IBD pathologist using standardised criteria. Tissues were preserved in RNAlater or flash-frozen (details in [online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-1}). Demographic and clinical characteristics are summarised in [online supplemental table 1](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC2){#xref-supplementary-material-2-1}, while [online supplemental table 2](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC3){#xref-supplementary-material-3-1} outlines the validation cohort design, including SMOC2 immunohistochemical analysis of stenotic tissues from 49 independent patients with CD.{#p-15}

### Mice and animal models {#sec-12}

*Gli1-CreERT2* , *Rosa26-tdTomato* , *Rosa26* -*iDTR* and *Smoc2-flox/flox* mice (Shanghai Model Organisms Center) were bred under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions. For lineage tracing, 7-8-week-old mice received tamoxifen (100 mg/kg/day, intraperitoneally, 4 days) 10 days before fibrosis induction. Chronic colitis and fibrosis were induced using established dextran sodium sulfate (DSS) (2%, 3 cycles) or 2,4,6-trinitrobenzene sulfonic acid (TNBS) (weekly intrarectal) protocols (details in [online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-2}). Fibrostenotic transformation was validated histologically (Masson's trichrome, immunohistochemistry (IHC)). Blinded histopathological scoring assessed fibrosis and inflammation.  

### SMART-Seq and bioinformatics analysis {#sec-13}

Single-cell suspensions from colons of DSS-treated *Gli1-CreERT2; R26-tdTomato* mice and controls were prepared and GLI1+ mesenchymal cells (CD45-CD31-CD326-tdTomato+) were sorted by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) (details in [online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-3}). Switching Mechanism at 5' End of RNA Template sequencing (SMART-Seq) libraries were prepared and sequenced on an Illumina NextSeq 500 platform. Raw data processing, alignment (GRCm39) and quantification were performed using TrimGalore, STAR and featureCounts. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between GLI1+ cells from fibrotic versus control mice were identified using DESeq2 (\|log2FC\|\>0.8, false discovery rate (FDR) \<0.05). Gene ontology (GO), gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) were performed to identify fibrosis-associated modules and hub genes (details in [online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-4}). *Smoc2* was prioritised as a key hub gene. Its expression correlation with fibrosis markers (eg, *Col1a2* , *Acta2* , *Postn*) was assessed across cohorts using Spearman correlation.  

### SMOC2 expression validation and clinical correlation {#sec-14}

SMOC2 expression in human and murine fibrotic and non-fibrotic tissues was quantified by quantitative PCR (qPCR) and immunofluorescence (IF)/IHC. Patients with CD were stratified into high- and low-expression groups based on SMOC2 messenger RNA (mRNA) (qPCR) or protein levels (quantitative IHC) in resected fibrotic tissues ([online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-5}). The cumulative probability of recurrence was analysed using Kaplan-Meier methods, with recurrence defined as endoscopic recurrence during follow-up.[25](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-25){#xref-ref-25-1} Logistic or Cox regression models were used to estimate ORs and HRs with adjustment for confounders ([online supplemental table 3](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC4){#xref-supplementary-material-4-1}). Predictive performance was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis using area under the curve (AUC).{#p-21}

### In vitro functional assays {#sec-15}

Primary human intestinal fibroblasts and FACS-isolated murine GLI1+ mesenchymal cells were cultured. Cells were transfected with *SMOC2/Smoc2* -specific or control small interfering RNA (siRNA) using Lipofectamine 3000. For functional assays, a scratch wound healing assay was performed with or without TGF-β (10 ng/mL) stimulation. The functional effects of *SMOC2/Smoc2* knockdown were further validated by qPCR and Western blot (WB) analysis (details in [online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-6}).  

### Histology, immunofluorescence and qPCR {#sec-16}

Human and murine tissues were fixed, embedded, sectioned and subjected to H\&E and Masson's trichrome staining. For IF, sections were stained with primary antibodies followed by Alexa Fluor-labelled secondaries (details in [online supplemental materials](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC1){#xref-supplementary-material-1-7}). Images were acquired by confocal microscopy (Olympus FV3000) and analysed with Imaris and ImageJ. A blinded, IBD-specialised pathologist performed histopathological scoring. Total RNA was extracted from intestinal tissues, reverse-transcribed and analysed by qPCR. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt^ method. Primer sequences are provided in [online supplemental table 4](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC5){#xref-supplementary-material-5-1}.{#p-24}

### Statistical analysis {#sec-17}

Data were analysed using R (V.4.2.3) or GraphPad Prism V.10. p\<0.05 was considered significant. ### GLI1+ mesenchymal cells are strongly associated with intestinal fibrogenesis {#sec-20}

To assess whether *GLI1* serves as a reliable marker for fibrosis-driving mesenchymal cells, we analysed single-cell RNA sequencing data from intestinal tissues of patients with CD fibrostenosis.[13](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-13){#xref-ref-13-2} *GLI1* was markedly upregulated in mesenchymal cells within fibrotic regions (p\<0.05) ([figure 1A,B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F1){#xref-fig-1-1}; [online supplemental figure 1A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC6){#xref-supplementary-material-6-1}), and IF of surgical samples confirmed increased GLI1 protein expression within fibrotic lesions ([figure 1C](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F1){#xref-fig-1-2}). Consistent results were observed in a chronic DSS-induced mouse model, in which single-cell RNA sequencing revealed *Gli1* upregulation in fibrotic tissue along with an increased abundance of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells ([figure 1D,E](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F1){#xref-fig-1-3}; [online supplemental figure 1B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC6){#xref-supplementary-material-6-2}).{#p-28}

### Ablation of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells attenuates intestinal fibrosis in mice {#sec-21}

To determine whether GLI1+ mesenchymal cells drive intestinal fibrosis, we employed a genetic ablation strategy using *Gli1-CreERT2; R26-iDTR* mice ([online supplemental figure 2A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC7){#xref-supplementary-material-7-1}), and generated *Gli1-CreERT2; R26-tdTomato; R26-iDTR* mice in parallel to validate the efficiency of in vivo cell ablation ([online supplemental figure 2B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC7){#xref-supplementary-material-7-2}). Following tamoxifen administration, mice received diphtheria toxin or phosphate-buffered saline on days 10, 12 and 14, followed by DSS or TNBS administration ([figure 2A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F2){#xref-fig-2-1}). IF and flow cytometry confirmed the efficient ablation of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells in this model ([figure 2B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F2){#xref-fig-2-2}; [online supplemental figure 2C,D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC7){#xref-supplementary-material-7-3}). Histological analysis and IF showed that GLI1+ mesenchymal cell ablation did not affect normal intestinal architecture but markedly reduced fibrosis severity, whereas inflammatory scores showed only a mild, non-significant decrease, consistent with a predominantly antifibrotic effect ([figure 2C--F](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F2){#xref-fig-2-3}). qPCR and WB analyses revealed significant downregulation of *Gli1* (p\<0.05), accompanied by reduced expression of fibrosis markers *Acta2* and *Fn1* ([figure 2G--J](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F2){#xref-fig-2-4}; [online supplemental figure 2E-G](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC7){#xref-supplementary-material-7-4}).{#p-33}

### GLI1+ mesenchymal cells exhibit a distinct fibrotic transcriptional signature {#sec-22}

To investigate the transcriptomic alterations underlying the fibrogenic potential of GLI1+ versus GLI1− mesenchymal cells, we isolated tdTomato+ (GLI1+) and tdTomato− (GLI1−) mesenchymal cells from water-treated and chronic DSS-treated mice using FACS and performed SMART-seq analysis ([figure 3A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F3){#xref-fig-3-1}). Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed marked transcriptional divergence between GLI1+ and GLI1− mesenchymal cells, with clear segregation of the two populations; PC1 accounted for 72% of the total variance ([online supplemental figure 3A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC8){#xref-supplementary-material-8-1}).{#p-36}

To further delineate the functional roles of GLI1+ and GLI1− mesenchymal cells under homeostatic and fibrotic conditions, we performed WGCNA to identify transcriptional modules associated with each cell subset ([figure 3B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F3){#xref-fig-3-2}, [online supplemental figure 3B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC8){#xref-supplementary-material-8-2}). To define the specific contribution of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells to intestinal fibrosis, we next performed DEG analyses across groups to identify fibrosis-related genes selectively upregulated in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells during fibrogenesis. Heatmap analysis revealed the enrichment of key profibrotic genes, including structural ECM components (*Col1a1* and *Col1a2* ), ECM regulators (*Mmp2* , *Postn* and *Smoc2* ) and inflammation-associated mediators (*Ptn* and *Tnfrsf11b* ) (p\<0.05, [figure 3F](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F3){#xref-fig-3-5}). On TGF-β stimulation, these genes were further induced in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells, highlighting their pivotal role in ECM production and regulation during intestinal fibrosis (p\<0.05, [figure 3G](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F3){#xref-fig-3-6}).  

### SMOC2 is a key profibrotic mediator specifically expressed by GLI1+ mesenchymal cells {#sec-23}

To identify the key mediators driving fibrosis in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells, we intersected genes from the GLI1+ DSS-associated module (yellow module) with all DEGs using a Venn diagram. Ranking the overlapping genes by Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (FPKM) values identified five top candidates (*Ptgdr* , *Cdh26* , *Smoc2* , *Ccdc85a* and *Zfp169* ). Among these, *Smoc2* showed strong positive correlations with multiple fibrosis-associated genes (all r\>0.55), supporting its potential relevance to GLI1+ mesenchymal cell-associated fibrogenesis ([figure 4A,B](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-1}, [online supplemental figure 4A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-1}).{#p-41}

The *Smoc2* gene encodes SPARC-related modular calcium-binding protein 2 (SMOC2), an ECM protein of the SPARC family.[26](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-26){#xref-ref-26-1} Single-cell RNA sequencing of DSS-induced mice confirmed that *Smoc2* was predominantly expressed in mesenchymal cells and was upregulated during fibrosis ([online supplemental figure 4B,C](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-2}). Confocal imaging further demonstrated increased SMOC2 expression in fibrotic regions colocalising with GLI1+ mesenchymal cells in chronic DSS and TNBS models ([figure 4C](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-2}). Following GLI1+ mesenchymal cell ablation, SMOC2 expression failed to increase after DSS treatment, indicating that SMOC2 upregulation during fibrosis depends on GLI1+ mesenchymal cells ([online supplemental figure 4D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-3}). qPCR analysis similarly showed elevated *Smoc2* levels in fibrotic tissues, which were significantly reduced in the absence of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells ([figure 4D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-3}, [online supplemental figure 4E](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-4}).{#p-44}

Analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing data from patients with fibrostenotic CD showed that *SMOC2* was predominantly expressed in mesenchymal cells and was upregulated during fibrosis ([online supplemental figure 4F,G](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-5}). Spatial transcriptomic analysis further demonstrated colocalisation of GLI1-high mesenchymal cells and *SMOC2* within fibrotic regions ([figure 4E](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-4}). Accordingly, GLI1-high module scores were positively correlated with *SMOC2* expression ([figure 4F](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-5}), whereas GLI1-low mesenchymal cells showed the opposite pattern ([online supplemental figure 4H,I](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-6}). Consistently, IF revealed increased SMOC2 expression in fibrotic areas colocalising with the myofibroblast marker αSMA ([figure 4G](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-6}; [online supplemental figure 4J](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC9){#xref-supplementary-material-9-7}), and WB of intestinal tissues confirmed significantly increased SMOC2 expression in fibrotic lesions ([figure 4H](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-7}). Furthermore, primary fibroblasts isolated from fibrotic intestinal regions of patients with CD exhibited higher baseline expression of fibrosis-related genes (*COL1A1* , *FN1* ) and *SMOC2* , which were further upregulated on TGF-β stimulation, supporting a profibrotic role of *SMOC2* in vitro ([figure 4I](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F4){#xref-fig-4-8}).  

### Therapeutic targeting of SMOC2 in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells attenuates intestinal fibrosis {#sec-24}

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of SMOC2 in intestinal fibrosis, we generated mice with GLI1+ mesenchymal cell-specific *Smoc2* conditional knockout (*Gli1-CreERT2; Smoc2-flox/flox* ) and subjected them to chronic DSS-induced fibrosis ([figure 5A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F5){#xref-fig-5-1}). Masson's trichrome staining and IF demonstrated markedly reduced fibrosis in *Smoc2* conditional knockout mice, accompanied by decreased SMOC2 expression and lower GLI1+ mesenchymal cell abundance ([figure 5B,C](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F5){#xref-fig-5-2}). Concordantly, qPCR revealed significant downregulation of *Smoc2* and key fibrogenic genes ([figure 5D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F5){#xref-fig-5-3}; [online supplemental figure 5A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC10){#xref-supplementary-material-10-1}). WB showed comparable whole-tissue baseline SMOC2 protein levels between control and *Smoc2* conditional knockout mice under homeostatic conditions. Following fibrosis induction, control mice exhibited robust upregulation of αSMA, fibronectin (FN) and SMOC2, whereas GLI1+ mesenchymal cell-specific *Smoc2* deletion markedly blunted SMOC2 induction and attenuated fibrotic deposition ([figure 5E,F](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F5){#xref-fig-5-4}). Collectively, these findings identify SMOC2 as a fibrosis-specific effector in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells that drives intestinal fibrosis progression.{#p-46}

### SMOC2 drives profibrotic functions of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells through autocrine and paracrine signalling {#sec-25}

Next, we sought to investigate the regulatory role of SMOC2 in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells during fibrosis. In vitro, primary murine GLI1+ and GLI1− mesenchymal cells exposed to TGF-β showed markedly elevated fibrotic marker expression in GLI1+ cells, accompanied by increased pSTAT3/STAT3 ratios ([figure 6A](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F6){#xref-fig-6-1}). Notably, siRNA-mediated knockdown of *Smoc2* in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells abolished TGF-β-induced STAT3 phosphorylation and FN upregulation ([figure 6B,C](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F6){#xref-fig-6-2}), indicating that these fibrotic responses are mediated by STAT3 via a SMOC2-dependent pathway. This regulatory axis was further validated in vivo. Stromal cells isolated from chronic DSS-treated *Gli1-CreERT2; Smoc2-flox/flox* mice exhibited markedly reduced *Smoc2* expression and profibrotic activity compared with those of the controls ([online supplemental figure 5D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC10){#xref-supplementary-material-10-3}). Similarly, intestinal tissues from mice subjected to chronic DSS or TNBS treatment with GLI1+ mesenchymal cell ablation showed decreased SMOC2 levels ([online supplemental figure 5E](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC10){#xref-supplementary-material-10-4}) and attenuated STAT3 phosphorylation, consistent with reduced intestinal fibrosis ([online supplemental figure 5F,G](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC10){#xref-supplementary-material-10-5}). Notably, fibrotic intestinal tissues from patients with CD displayed elevated pSTAT3/STAT3 ratios, supporting the clinical relevance of this SMOC2-dependent signalling pathway ([online supplemental figure 5H,I](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC10){#xref-supplementary-material-10-6}).{#p-50}

Given that SMOC2 is a secreted protein, we next quantified its expression in primary cells and found it to be secreted at higher levels by GLI1+ mesenchymal cells than by GLI1− cells ([figure 6D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F6){#xref-fig-6-3}). ### SMOC2 serves as a prognostic biomarker of postoperative recurrence in fibrostenotic CD {#sec-26}

Building on the therapeutic relevance of SMOC2, we next evaluated its potential as a biomarker for predicting postoperative recurrence in a cohort of patients with CD-associated fibrostenosis. Examination of paired fibrotic and non-fibrotic surgical specimens from a discovery cohort (n=134) revealed significantly elevated SMOC2 expression in fibrotic regions, as assessed by both IHC and qPCR ([figure 7A--C](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F7){#xref-fig-7-1}; [online supplemental figure 5J](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#DC10){#xref-supplementary-material-10-7}). Furthermore, postoperative patients with available endoscopic evaluation at 6-12 months were stratified according to Rutgeerts score as low (\<2), intermediate (2--3) and severe (4). Notably, in the intermediate and severe groups, SMOC2 expression remained significantly higher in fibrotic than in non-fibrotic tissue and was greater than that observed in the low-recurrence group ([figure 7D](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#F7){#xref-fig-7-2}).{#p-53}

Discussion {#sec-27}
--------------------

In this study, we identified GLI1+ mesenchymal cells as key mediators of intestinal fibrostenosis in CD. Lineage tracing combined with FACS-sorted transcriptomic analyses revealed that GLI1+ mesenchymal cells selectively expanded within fibrotic lesions and exhibited a distinct profibrotic transcriptional programme. SMOC2 was identified as a GLI1+ mesenchymal cell-specific effector driving ECM deposition through autocrine amplification and paracrine activation of neighbouring fibroblasts. Genetic deletion or siRNA-mediated silencing of *Smoc2* markedly attenuated fibrogenic activation both in vivo and in vitro. Clinically, SMOC2 was upregulated in fibrotic tissues and associated with postoperative recurrence, outperforming conventional fibrotic markers and highlighting its dual potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.{#p-56}

Our study extends previous findings of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells as conserved profibrotic mediators across organs. These cells have been implicated in tissue remodelling via mTOR,[27](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-27){#xref-ref-27-1} MAPK[28](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-28){#xref-ref-28-1} and JAK/STAT[29](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-29){#xref-ref-29-1} signalling and secretion of profibrotic factors such as ADAMTS12. In the intestine, we systematically characterised the functional heterogeneity between GLI1+ and GLI1− mesenchymal cells using SMART-seq profiling. WGCNA analysis showed that GLI1+ mesenchymal cells maintain ECM homeostasis under steady state, but shift toward enhanced ECM remodelling and TGF-β associated profibrotic signalling during fibrosis, consistent with patterns observed in other organs.[29](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-29){#xref-ref-29-2} Prior studies demonstrating MSC-like properties of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells and fibrosis attenuation on their ablation are consistent with our findings.[16 21](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-16){#xref-ref-16-4} Recent single-cell and spatial transcriptomic studies have further refined the stromal landscape of intestinal fibrosis. Spatial analyses have delineated region-specific fibrotic niches and cellular heterogeneity,[30 31](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-30){#xref-ref-30-1} while integrative single-cell and spatial profiling has identified GLIS3 as a regulator of fibrotic remodelling.[32](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-32){#xref-ref-32-1} Collectively, these findings support a broader GLI/GLIS-associated transcriptional network in intestinal fibrosis. Within this context, our study functionally identifies SMOC2 as a key downstream effector of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells, defining a GLI1-SMOC2 axis that drives fibrotic remodelling.{#p-57}

In-depth transcriptional profiling revealed *Smoc2* as a key gene selectively and robustly upregulated under fibrotic conditions. SMOC2, a member of the SPARC family, is known to regulate cell-matrix interactions.[26](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-26){#xref-ref-26-2} SMOC family proteins are broadly involved in embryonic development,[33](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-33){#xref-ref-33-1} cell-cycle regulation,[34](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-34){#xref-ref-34-1} adhesion and migration,[35](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-35){#xref-ref-35-1} fibrosis,[36 37](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-36){#xref-ref-36-1} calcification,[38](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-38){#xref-ref-38-1} angiogenesis[39](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-39){#xref-ref-39-1} and tumourigenesis.[40 41](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-40){#xref-ref-40-1} SMOC2 is abundantly expressed across multiple tissues, predominantly localised within the ECM and basement membrane, where it promotes fibril assembly and can also serve as a marker of intestinal stem cells.[42](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-42){#xref-ref-42-1} We found that SMOC2 was predominantly expressed in stromal compartments and strongly upregulated in both murine and human fibrotic intestinal tissues. Mechanistic studies identified SMOC2 as a key driver of fibroblast activation: in vitro silencing of *Smoc2* in GLI1+ mesenchymal cells attenuated TGF-β induced fibrogenic markers and STAT3 phosphorylation, while in vivo ablation of GLI1+ mesenchymal cells or conditional deletion of *Smoc2* significantly reduced collagen deposition. Notably, SMOC2 promoted fibrosis through dual mechanisms: sustaining GLI1+ mesenchymal cell activation in an autocrine manner while inducing a profibrotic phenotype in neighbouring fibroblasts via paracrine signalling, thereby establishing a feed-forward loop. Unlike SPARC, which directly binds collagen to regulate fibril assembly,[26 43](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-26){#xref-ref-26-3} SMOC2 lacks collagen-binding motifs, suggesting distinct extracellular signalling mechanisms in fibrosis.{#p-58}

Recent studies further support a pathogenic role of SMOC2 across organs. It promotes fibroblast activation in renal fibrosis,[36 44](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-36){#xref-ref-36-2} while *Smoc2* −/− mice genetic deletion reduces pulmonary fibrosis.[37](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-37){#xref-ref-37-1} Moreover, in rheumatoid arthritis and osteogenesis, SMOC2 promotes cell migration and bone formation through transcriptional regulation, m6A modification and integrin-related signalling pathways.[45 46](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-45){#xref-ref-45-1} Elevated circulating SMOC2 levels have also been detected in patients with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, highlighting its potential as a liquid biopsy biomarker.[47](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-47){#xref-ref-47-1} In patients with CD-associated fibrostenosis, SMOC2 emerges as a potential prognostic biomarker for postoperative recurrence. Both SMOC2 mRNA and IHC quantification demonstrated superior predictive performance compared with *COL1A1* and *FN1* , with higher SMOC2 levels correlating with an increased recurrence risk. ROC analysis further confirmed that SMOC2 IHC was the most reliable predictor (discovery cohort AUC 0.83; independent validation cohort AUC 0.81), supporting its translational relevance and potential utility for biomarker-guided risk stratification in CD.[48](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-48){#xref-ref-48-1}{#p-59}

Although our findings suggest that SMOC2 may act as an important contributor to fibrosis driven by GLI1+ mesenchymal cells, several limitations exist. First, DSS and TNBS models may not fully recapitulate human intestinal fibrosis and are influenced by inflammation. Second, other stromal cell populations and signalling pathways may also contribute to fibrogenesis, which were not comprehensively investigated here. Third, the sample size of the external validation cohort was relatively small, underscoring the need for larger multicentre studies, which could be further strengthened by incorporating preoperative and postoperative plasma samples.[49](https://gut.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/08/gutjnl-2025-337952#ref-49){#xref-ref-49-1} Finally, the long-term efficacy and safety of SMOC2-targeted interventions remain to be established, warranting further pharmacological evaluation.{#p-60}

In summary, GLI1+ mesenchymal cells represent a key pathogenic population in CD fibrostenosis, with SMOC2 acting as a central effector regulating ECM deposition and fibrogenesis through autocrine and paracrine mechanisms. SMOC2 is a promising biomarker of postoperative recurrence and a potential therapeutic target. Future studies should focus on pharmacological inhibition of SMOC2, longitudinal validation in large patient cohorts and combined strategies targeting multiple stromal pathways to prevent or reverse the progression of fibrostenosis in CD.{#p-61}
